         Buckthorn (and honeysuckle) sampling

Background information
Today, we are going out into the field to sample invasive species that are affecting woodlands
around Western’s campus. Below, you can see the area we will be sampling (from the university
bridge to Western Alumni field). Our primary goals will be to (1) systematically sample the area
for the presence of buckthorn and honeysuckle and (2) identify areas where large buckthorn
trees are growing to prioritize their removal (haphazard sampling).




Health and Safety
We are going to be working off-trail in a woodland area near the river. Please watch your step,
be careful of overhanging branches and thorns, and be mindful of uneven and slippery terrain.
Some of the areas we will be working in will be damp and wet so make sure you have solid
footing. Do not approach or go into the river, the fast-moving waters can be dangerous. Keep at
least a 5 m buffer away from any moving water while you are sampling. If you are unable to
safely sample one of your plots, please inform the instructor. Do not conduct any sampling in
areas that may be unsafe. If you are injured, please let the instructor know immediately.

Phase II: Identifying high seed producers
Background information:
Buckthorn has an incredibly high rate of seed germination and the largest seed producers can
substantially contribute to the expansion of an invasion. Therefore, identifying the largest
producers for removal can have a disproportionate effect on invasive species spread.
Haphazard sampling is a form of sampling where we move along an area and search for our
item of interest without any randomization or systematic plan. We will be moving through the
wooded area together trying to identify any areas where there are large buckthorn tees, which
we will classify as trees that are greater than 25 cm in circumference at the standard height of
1.4 metres. We will then be recording the GPS coordinates of each tree that reaches that
threshold as we move along.
