# Animals on the Move: Tracking Animal Movement Across Seascapes

---

![](../sources/files/IMG_5011.JPG)

Understanding the causes of animal movement can help us better predict when and where animals will encounter threats and risks. However, animal movement is complex, often driven by a multitude of internal and external factors, and particularly challenging to study in marine environments.

In this lesson, we will explore some of the most common technologies used to track animals across landscapes and seascapes, including satellite tags, acoustic arrays, and biologgers. We'll focus on basking sharks as a model species to examine how tracking data can reveal migratory pathways, habitat preferences, and risk exposure in ocean ecosystems.

**Environmental Analysis Learning Objectives**

- Apply filtering techniques across Excel, R, and QGIS to extract relevant subsets of data for analysis
- Restructure, calculate, and summarize datasets using appropriate tools in Excel, R, and QGIS to prepare data for analysis
- Import, process, and manipulate raster and vector datasets in QGIS

**Lesson outline**

1. Review of pre-class work and homework assignment (30 minutes)
2. Sourcing openly available data and data type availability (30 minutes)
3. Animals on the Move: Tracking Animal Movement Across Seascapes lecture (30 minutes)
4. Positron: Data Wrangling (1 hour)
5. Case study: Marine sportfishing tagging programme (1 hour)
