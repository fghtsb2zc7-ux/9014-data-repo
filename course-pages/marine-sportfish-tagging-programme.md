## Background

Citizen science is a growing source of reliable data for many groups of species. Portals include:

- <https://ebird.org/home>
- <https://www.inaturalist.org/>

In this case study, we're going to look at [The Marine Sportfish Tagging Programme](https://www.fisheriesireland.ie/research/marine-sportfish-tagging-programme) established by Inland Fisheries Ireland. Here is a quick overview of the data:

- Established in 1970 to promote catch-and-release of elasmobranchs (sharks, skates, rays) at a time when most catches were kept.
- One of the longest-running tagging programmes of its kind worldwide.
- Over 50 years, more than 40,000 fish tagged and over 3,000 recaptures recorded.
- Data has revealed migration patterns, local extinctions (e.g., angelshark), and seasonal site fidelity in species like juvenile porbeagle.
- Citizen science encouraged — anglers can report tagged fish

## Case Study Objectives

The following are some open-ended objectives I want you and your team to work on.

You are preparing a short briefing for program managers at Inland Fisheries Ireland on the citizen-science tagging program. Use the dataset to show how the program has developed over time, who and what is contributing to the records, and any important temporal or species-level patterns you identify.

Use well-chosen visualizations and a concise narrative to highlight the most important findings. Conclude by considering what your results might mean for the future of the program, including potential priorities for monitoring, outreach, or resourcing.
