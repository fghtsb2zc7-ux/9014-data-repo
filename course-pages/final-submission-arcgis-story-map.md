### Project description (copied from course outline)

The final deliverable for your project is a comprehensive ArcGIS storymap that highlights the results of your analysis and provides interpretation by effectively integrating text, figures, maps, and tables into one flowing document. We have scheduled an elective ArcGIS storymaps and dashboards workshop for you on **November 6th** and unless you feel very confident using storymaps and dashboards  
already, I would strongly recommend that you attend that workshop. Storymaps are a fun and interactive way to share data visualizations (we’ll review examples throughout the course) and this project will give you (1) practice on how to best use storymaps and dashboards and (2) a final deliverable you can share with potential co-op employers

- Infographic - You will create a `social media ready` infographic that provides an overview and summary of your analysis for the public. You can prepare this infographic in something like PowerPoint or Canva and then include it as a picture within your overall StoryMap.  Your infographic should be something engaging that conveys the main, high-level findings of your project (i.e. it does not need to include all of your findings).

  - I've provided some examples from previous infographics below. Please note, if you use figures or maps in your infographic, please make sure they are readable in your infographic (e.g., increase the font size relative to the figure size).
    - [Bird\_infographic.png](../sources/files/Infographics/Bird_infographic.png)
    - [Climate infographic.png](<../sources/files/Infographics/Climate infographic.png>)
    - [august complex infographic.pdf](<../sources/files/Infographics/august complex infographic.pdf>)
- Figures – You will need 10 figures created using R
- Maps - 10 maps created in QGIS (with at least two interactive maps). For your interactive maps, you will be using spatial data you processed within QGIS, but are just displaying it in an interactive format within StoryMaps (i.e. not a static image). Your dashboard can count towards one of your interactive maps.
- Dashboard - at least one dashboard created within your ArcGIS StoryMap
- Written summary - A written interpretation to go along with your data visualizations and analysis that summarizes your key findings. Make sure to cite all figures and maps that you include. While interpreting your results be sure to incorporate references from academic literature to provide context; however, the emphasis should be primarily on your analysis and interpretation of the open-source data you have gathered. Your written summary should not exceed 2000 words.
- A list of data sources - Provide a list of data sources you used for your project with weblinks to each of them.
- References section - include any academic references you have cited in your assignment. Use APA 7th edition for your reference format.

  All figures, graphs, or maps should be created (by you) from data you have collected from various open sources.
