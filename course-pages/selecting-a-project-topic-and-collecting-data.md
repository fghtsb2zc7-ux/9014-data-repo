# Selecting a project topic and collecting data

*Module: Assessments > Independent Project *

# Independent Project

---

The major project for this course is an independent data analysis assignment where you'll explore a topic of your choice. This project is intentionally open-ended, offering you the flexibility to define a reasonable scope, source relevant datasets, and conduct meaningful analyses.  It challenges you to thoughtfully plan and execute your data exploration from start to finish. A defining aspect of this project is the sourcing of data from open-source databases and repositories.

You'll take the lead in combining and analyzing datasets to uncover meaningful relationships and answer the key questions you've identified for your project.

## Selecting a project topic

Based on your interests and career ambitions, you need to select a broad topic related to ecosystem health and it will be your responsibility to answer questions around the topic using temporal and spatial environmental data.  As you are highly dependent upon the data available, I would strongly recommend that you identify a general, high-level topic first and then search for datasets available around that topic. You should avoid clearly identifying a set of research questions for your analysis before first ensuring that are enough available datasets to support that project topic.  Here are a few examples of topics students have chosen in the past:

- Southwestern Ontario: Ecosystem Health in the Presence of Neonicotinoids
- Analyzing Air Quality near Emitting Facilities in Ontario
- The Impact of Fossil Fuel Energy for Electricity Generation on Ecosystem Health

Critically, I think it is of utmost importance to choose a topic that resonates with you and aligns with your goals for this course and program, as well as your co-op interests. Many students have referenced their Independent projects in interviews or even shared their projects with potential employers.

## How many datasets do I need to find?

Alright, one of the most challenging and frustrating components of this project is trying to find data for your topic.  Each of you will have a specific topic. In some cases, information on that topic is compiled into large complex datasets whereas in other cases, the data may be spread out amongst several different ones. Therefore, there is no single correct answer for how many datasets each of you should find.  In your initial data exploration phase, you should attempt to find as many relevant datasets as you can in the beginning and then whittle things down to more refined questions once you understand what is available.
